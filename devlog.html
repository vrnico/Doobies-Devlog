Aug 3, 2026

I burned a month of tokens in two days

Spent Friday and Saturday running a whole fleet of agents at the party game and blew essentially my entire month's AI budget doing it. What came out the other side: 134 passing tests, 27 pieces of 3D art, a working coin economy, event spaces that roll three dice at once, and multiplayer that actually holds together.

Then I sat back and realised the number of human beings who knew any of it existed was zero. I did the expensive part and skipped the free part. So — this. Hello.

Next: record thirty seconds of the doobies moving and put it somewhere a stranger can see it.

Aug 1, 2026

I deleted a whole minigame the same day I liked one

Two minigames landed on the same day. One of them, Colour Claim, is the first thing in this project's history I've liked without being asked. The other one, Bumper Pit, I killed on sight. Here's what I actually said about it, and I'm not softening it:

"It's not even really a game, there's no doobies in it, it's top down, it feels like there's no user control, it's really poorly made."

The handoff notes claimed it was verified and working. It was neither. That's the whole lesson: a clean compile and passing tests do not mean a human being would enjoy touching it. So the rule now is that nothing counts as done until somebody has actually played it — real characters, a camera you can play from, working controls.

Next: figure out whether stealing a tile feels different from painting a bare one. That's the entire design target and I still don't know.
