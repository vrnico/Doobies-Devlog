What it actually is

A board you race, and a minigame at the end of every round.

You roll, you land on something, something happens to you — usually expensive. Then everybody gets dumped into a minigame and the money moves again. The goal is a dub. Doinks are just how you get one.

Who's in it

Two of them want your money.

The hustlerSquirma

Tall, yellow, very long arms, red bikini. She'll sell you a dub for twelve doinks and she will always come out ahead. The rule I wrote for her is that she's never cruel — the second she sounds like she enjoys hurting you she stops being funny. She's generous to whoever's losing and greedy toward whoever's winning, and it's the same line either way.

The markYour friends

Four to ten of them, over the internet or sat next to you. One types a code, everyone rolls for turn order, and then the board decides who's still speaking at the end. Empty seats fill with CPU players who have names and, regrettably, opinions.

Aug 3, 2026NetcodeShipped

It's called Doobies, and it plays over the internet

The networked scene used to be a connection test with doobies standing in it. It's the actual game now — real board, real spaces, real minigame ladder — behind a title screen you can type your name into and a code you can join with. Turn order gets rolled online instead of decided by whoever loaded first.

Then I made a real build and found the art in all six minigames was editor-only, so every one of them shipped completely empty. It had been true for days. The editor was always right there being green at me.

Next: put a build in someone else's hands and watch them fail to connect. It's the only test left that means anything.
Aug 1, 2026Rejected

Squeem got a skeleton and Bumper Pit got deleted

Squeem — the casino man — went from a measured mesh to thirteen bones and nine animation clips in a day, and I signed off all nine. Three more minigames landed the same day: Color Call, Color Dial, Chopper Run. A fourth, Bumper Pit, I killed on sight:

"It's not even really a game, there's no doobies in it, it's top down, it feels like there's no user control, it's really poorly made."

Its notes said it was verified and working. It was neither. Nothing counts as done here now until a human has actually played it.

Next: work out whether stealing a tile feels different from painting a bare one. That's the whole design target and I still don't know.
Jul 31, 2026First post

I pointed everything at it at once and burned a month of tokens

One day: a minigame framework with a seeded contract under it, the first minigame on top of that, Squirma turning up at her own spaces to react to every landing, and a HUD that survives ten players. Also every sound effect in the game existed and not one of them fired, and the agents were all sharing a single git index, so they kept committing each other's half-finished work.

Then I sat back and realised the number of people who knew any of it existed was zero. I did the expensive part and skipped the free part. So — this. Hello.

Next: record thirty seconds of the doobies moving and put it somewhere a stranger can see it.

The one ask

Come and break it with your friends.

A party game can't be tested by one person. I need groups — four to ten people in a room or on a call — willing to play something half-finished and then tell me the truth about it.

What you're signing up for

  • A build, sent to you when there's one worth playing. Windows first.
  • One session, whenever suits you — 30 to 60 minutes with your own people.
  • Telling me honestly what was boring, confusing, or annoying. Especially boring.
  • No spam, no newsletter, no "Doobies weekly." I'll only email you about playing.
