What it is

A board you race, and a minigame every round.

You roll, you land on something, and it usually costs you. Then everyone drops into a minigame and the money moves again. First to a dub wins. Doinks are how you get one.

Who's in it

Two of them want your money.

The hustlerSquirma

Tall, yellow, very long arms, red bikini. She sells dubs for twelve doinks and she always comes out ahead. Generous to whoever is losing, greedy toward whoever is winning. Same line either way.

The markYour friends

Four to ten of them, online or sat next to you. One person hosts, everyone rolls for turn order, and the board sorts out the rest. Empty seats fill with CPU players.

Aug 3, 2026Netcode

It's called Doobies, and it plays over the internet

The networked scene used to be a connection test with doobies standing in it. It's the real game now, behind a title screen you can type a name into and a code you can join with. Turn order gets rolled online instead of decided by who loaded first.

Then I made a real build and found the art in all six minigames was editor only, so every one of them shipped empty. It had been true for days.

Next: put a build in someone else's hands and watch them fail to connect.
Aug 1, 2026Rejected

Squeem got a skeleton, Bumper Pit got deleted

Squeem, the casino guy, went from a measured mesh to thirteen bones and nine animation clips in a day. Three more minigames landed: Color Call, Color Dial, Chopper Run. A fourth, Bumper Pit, I killed on sight:

"It's not even really a game, there's no doobies in it, it's top down, it feels like there's no user control, it's really poorly made."

Its notes said it was verified. It wasn't. Nothing counts as done here until someone has played it.

Next: work out whether stealing a tile feels different from painting a bare one.
Jul 31, 2026First post

Everything at once

One day: a minigame framework, the first minigame on top of it, Squirma turning up at her own spaces to react to every landing, and a HUD that holds ten players. Also every sound effect existed and none of them fired, and the agents shared one git index so they kept committing each other's work.

Then I realised nobody outside this machine knew any of it existed. So, this.

Next: record thirty seconds of the doobies moving and put it somewhere a stranger can see it.
